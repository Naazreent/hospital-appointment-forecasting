A forecast can work well for two years and still struggle when the system changes.

I’ve been working on a healthcare analytics mini project using NHS England’s historical outpatient activity data. I compared five forecasting approaches using time-based validation, rather than randomly splitting the data.

Ridge regression reduced mean absolute error by 19.9% compared with using the same month from the previous year on the 2018 to 2019 test period. But its error increased sharply during the 2020 stress period.

That was the useful lesson for me: a good historical score doesn’t tell us everything about how a forecast behaves under disruption. The project includes reproducible code, saved forecasts, uncertainty bands and checks for future-data leakage.

These figures measure recorded first outpatient attendances, not unmet demand. This is a historical research demonstration, not a live hospital planning tool.
